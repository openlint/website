summary: |
  The fifth OpenLint office hours took stock of a project that now feels open for business, and turned to how rules are written, organized and shared.

  **Status.** OpenLint has an npm package, a docs site for the tools ([docs.openlint.org](https://docs.openlint.org)) and a spec site ([spec.openlint.org](https://spec.openlint.org)). Miguel Quintero's TypeScript 7 upgrade has merged. The Dutch government team is moving its tooling from Spectral to OpenLint and announcing it on its blog, and Kin has switched his own tools. The aim is an official launch at API Days Paris, with a few migration stories behind it.

  **Rulesets and education.** Jakub Rożek proposed moving the built-in rulesets out of the main repo, because Spectral's tests were coupled to them and small rule changes broke unrelated tests. Joost Farla asked for a standard way to organize rulesets across several standards and versions, and for modular, reusable parts, like JSON Schema references. The group's biggest friction is writing the regular expressions and JSONPath for a rule; Dimitri's example was a `servers` rule that also has to handle response bodies and callbacks. The plan is to start lean: publish common snippets on openlint.org, document how rules, inheritance and aliases work today, and grow that into an education section and, later, a registry of rules, aliases, regex and JSONPath that the spec and skills can reference, ideally co-owned with standards bodies such as OpenAPI, AsyncAPI, Arazzo, PSD2 and FHIR.

  **Agents.** Agents will write most rules, so the knowledge has to be published where models and tools can find it. Miguel will look at getting OpenLint into Context7. Dimitri described the Dutch "validator in the loop", where a skill lints the ruleset an agent writes, which fits the [MCP server (#13)](https://github.com/openlint/openlint/issues/13). Joost described an AI-ready standards project in the Netherlands.

  **Editors and roadmap.** People are waiting on the VS Code extension. Dimitri and Joost offered to look at a language server, alongside Ivan's extension. Markdown and agent-skill linting is a top roadmap item. Dimitri will compare vacuum's rules with OpenLint's.

  **Spec and funding.** The working draft is in good shape. Kin has small PRs open, and Joost will iterate next week. Open Source Europe is the fiscal host, with $1,000 in the account. Kin is preparing Sovereign Tech Fund and other European grant proposals, including the test harness and education, and asked for core-tooling ideas.
