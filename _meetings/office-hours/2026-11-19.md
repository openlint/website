summary: |
  The eighth OpenLint office hours agreed that the specification should drive the toolbox, not the other way round, and that this is December's priority, ahead of new toolbox features. A separate spec meeting and a chat space for the project are coming.

  **Status.** Two toolbox releases this week: `openlint@1.2.0` split the rulesets into packages and added the opt-in schema cache from SAP's Open Resource Discovery team, and `openlint@1.3.0` pinned the `spectral:*` compatibility rulesets at 1.0.0. The spec is at 1.0. A new `roadmap` label on [openlint/openlint](https://github.com/openlint/openlint/issues?q=label%3Aroadmap) collects the bugs and housekeeping that are up next, and toolbox changes are on the [changelog](https://openlint.org/changelog/).

  **MCP server.** Miguel Quintero's [`@openlint/openlint-mcp`](https://github.com/openlint/mcp) offers five read-only tools: list rulesets, validate a ruleset, lint a document, list rules and explain a rule. Its docs are on Context7. The first outside pull request reports slow linting on large documents (62 seconds for a 2.7 MB OpenAPI document, past most clients' 60-second timeout) and proposes a size limit. Miguel will merge it and then freeze until after apidays Paris.

  **Performance and document size.** Kin will lead performance benchmarks, so that merges like the schema cache are measured, not just trusted. Mike Kistler pointed out that how split OpenAPI documents compose (where a referenced security scheme is resolved, for example) is still ambiguous and worth raising with OpenAPI. Kin sees rulesets describing different document "shapes" that organizations can choose from.

  **How the project is organized.** The diagram on [openlint.org](https://openlint.org) shows the roles, repos and how changes land ([community#41](https://github.com/openlint/community/issues/41)). Joost Farla asked for clear rules on who approves and merges pull requests. Each lead sets their repo's rules first; Kin will propose a baseline (such as two reviewers) next week. Andre said it's hard to follow what's happening and how to contribute, and suggested dedicated sessions per area.

  **Communication.** The project will get its own chat space with channels per area. The APIs You Won't Hate invite failed for Mike. Kin will compare Slack, Discord and Matrix on cost and accessibility for a vote next week.

  **The spec drives the toolbox.** Joost is finding behaviour the spec now states that neither OpenLint nor Spectral implements (alphabetical ordering, cycles through `extends`), so the conformance tests fail, and Spectral can't be fixed from here. Kin's position: the spec is authoritative, OpenLint fixes its bugs, and the project files issues on Spectral and vacuum. Today the spec is checked against the toolbox to keep the Spectral promise; once the spec is stable, the toolbox's CI should fail when it breaks the spec. New features, such as the schema cache's new option, should start in the spec. The pipeline spans the spec, conformance and the toolbox, with downstream builds like the MCP server. Joost will propose a time for a weekly spec meeting, possibly kicking off in person in Paris.

  **apidays Paris (1–3 December).** Talks from Matthias Keckl, Kin, Dimitri van Hees, and Miguel (on API developer experience, Wednesday 2 December). Miguel is writing an article for apidays on why OpenLint. The logo concepts in [community#47](https://github.com/openlint/community/issues/47) are open for votes. Kin, Phil Sturgeon and Joost will join the Nordic APIs LiveCast in January. Next office hours, 26 November, is the last before Paris.
