summary: |
  The sixth OpenLint office hours confirmed the project is ready for pilots, and reset the roadmap's priorities: compatibility and trust before new features.

  **Status.** `openlint` 1.1.3 is on npm and behaves like Spectral. Miguel Quintero finished the TypeScript 7 work and starts the [MCP server (#13)](https://github.com/openlint/openlint/issues/13) this weekend, with a demo planned for next week. The Learn section now has its own site, [learn.openlint.org](https://learn.openlint.org), for deeper guides on rulesets, `extends` and aliases, while the docs stay minimal. [rules.openlint.org](https://rules.openlint.org) is a generated catalog with one page per official rule. The blog has posts on migrating, the fiscal host, aliases and the Dutch government's move, plus an RSS feed. A second donation came in, from Steve Willmott.

  **Rules architecture.** Kin presented [openlint#40](https://github.com/openlint/openlint/issues/40): how formats, rulesets and standards fit together, including MCP, A2A, agent skills, CAMARA, TM Forum and FHIR. He raised a concern: most teams use the default ruleset unchanged, so modularity shouldn't confuse them. One idea was a "batteries included" default with extra rules ready to switch on.

  **Compatibility first.** Dimitri van Hees explained that the Spectral ruleset is written into the Dutch API Design Rules standard. Every Dutch government organization that used Spectral now uses OpenLint, but many people use vacuum. Because vacuum isn't fully compatible, Go users copy the standard's ruleset. He asked to prioritize a next version of the spec that works with both Spectral and vacuum, even as a draft that people can test in pipelines. He'll write up the differences as a matrix. The main one: Spectral relies on JavaScript's JSONPath Plus and regular expressions, where vacuum follows the standards. `x-` properties, such as a rule category, are a way to add features without breaking compatibility. Kin will build a conformance suite with a visual dashboard of how OpenLint, Spectral, vacuum and other tools line up, before anything new is added. He'll also reach out to SmartBear, Redocly and other tooling vendors.

  **Performance.** Nangiale Shir of Open Resource Discovery (SAP), invited by Pavel Kornev, uses Spectral at the core of a validator that runs large pipelines. What matters most to them is performance, and how quickly the community reviews their opt-in caching PR ([#39](https://github.com/openlint/openlint/pull/39)). Kin will add a performance and housekeeping section to the roadmap and review the PR.

  **Funding and governance.** The Dutch standardization board wants to fund maintainers in next year's budget, possibly by freeing up dedicated people rather than paying Open Source Europe directly. Kin is writing grant proposals for the conformance suite, the spec, Learn and rulesets. OpenLint is ready for pilots and design partners now, with the official launch at API Days Paris in December.
